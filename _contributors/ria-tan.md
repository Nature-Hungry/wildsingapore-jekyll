Ria Tan started her conservation journey about 30 years ago with the Chek Jawa reclamation crisis. She wandered the shores, trying to document all lifeforms present before it would get buried and reclaimed. But the land reclamation was deferred and today we still have Chek Jawa, thanks to our pioneers in conservation who stood up for our biodiversity who have no voice. Read more about the [Chek Jawa Reclamation here](/reclamation/tekong-ubin/index.html).

It all began with the website [Wild Singapore](https://wildsingapore.com), which accompanied the Chek Jawa Guide Book. Starting small, she added bits of content over time. After three decades, the end result was a very large website acting as a massive repository of information regarding all aspects of our Singaporean shores.

Ria still wanders on our shores till today, documenting everything she and her team finds over at the [Wild Shores Blog](https://wildshores.blogspot.com).

Without Ria, the conception of this website would not be possible. She still guides the Nature Hungry team and steers this project.
