Freshwater bodies form when the water table is higher than the substrate. In Singapore, our largest freshwater bodies like our reservoirs, are artificial. The first reservoir created in Singapore is the Macritchie Reservoir, which was created in 1867.

## Biodiversity and Reservoirs

Reservoirs are large surfaces of water, with nothing above the water. After the creation of reservoirs within our Central Catchment Nature Reserve (CCNR), habitat fragmentation happened and an extinction debt was created. Creating the reservoirs meant logging and removing all trees below the projected waterline, and this removed the ability for less-mobile species to travel between the CCNR. The White-chested Babbler populations on our mainland have died off due to extensive inbreeding, caused by [habitat fragmentation](/articles/concepts/habitatfragmentation/).

Singapore is one of little countries with potable tap water, but this is not without sacrifices. The Public Utilities Board (PUB) likes to keep the water within these reservoirs squeaky clean to reduce cost on filtering and processing of the raw water, as it gets transformed into our potable tap water. This means that reservoirs have relatively little biomass within the water body. No weeds, wetland plants, floating plants, or any trees. Because of such extensive measures by PUB, reservoirs have very little biodiversity in general.
