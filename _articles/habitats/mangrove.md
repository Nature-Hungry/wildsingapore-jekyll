## Plants Commonly Found Here

### **Main characterising plants**

- Avicennia spp. (Usually front mangrove)
- Rhizophora spp.
- Brugeria spp. (Usually back mangrove)
- Nipah Palm (*Nypa fruticans*)

There are a lot more mangrove species. Globally, there are 70 species, 35 of which are in Singapore. There are also non-mangrove trees which are associated with the mangrove habitat. All these plants have adapted to growing in the tidal zone, with high salinity and anoxic muddy substrate.

Traits of these adaptations include:
- secretion of salt on the leaves
- a wide root base for stability
- breathing points above the waterline
    - descenders from high branches
    - prop/stilt roots
    - lenticels
    - pneumatophores

## Animals Commonly Found Here

### **Main characterising animals**

- Black-and-red Broadbill
- Copper-throated Sunbird
- Ruby-cheeked Sunbird
- Mangrove Pitta

Mangroves have mangrove specialists. These animals live exclusively in the mangroves, and mangrove conservation is required to keep these animals extant.

### Other Animals Found Here

- Brown-throated Sunbird
- House Crow

## Forest Succession

Mangrove, unlike terrestrial forests, do NOT need to undergo forest succession. There are no primary or secondary mangrove forests, and mangrove trees are sorted by zonation to allow for different species to live in different functional niches instead.
