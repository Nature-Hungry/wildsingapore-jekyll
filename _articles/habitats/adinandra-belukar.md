## Why the Name?

Adinandra belukar means "Bush of Adinandra". [*Adinandra dumosa*](/entries/plants/coastal/adinandra/dumosa/), also known as the 'Tiup-tiup', is the main plant species in this habitat's characterisation.

## Plants Commonly Found Here

### **Main characterising plants**
- [Tiup-tiup (*Adinandra dumosa*)](/entries/plants/coastal/adinandra/dumosa/)
- [Resam Fern (*Dicranopteris linearis*)](/entries/plants/tracheophyta/polypodiopsida/gleicheniales/gleicheniaceae/dicranopteris/linearis/)

These plants, if covering the land in abundance, would tell you that you are standing in an Adinandra Belukar habitat.

### Other **associated** Plants
- *Smilax setosa*
- *Fragraea fragrans*
- *Campnosperma auriculatum*
- *Macaranga gigantea*
- *Macaranga heynei*

These plants are associated with Adinandra belukar habitats, but are not a tell-tale sign. These plants are all native, with all except the first being trees. The presence of these trees show that the habitat you're in is quite early in the process of succession.

## Animals Commonly Found Here
- Pin-striped Tit Babbler
- Rufous Woodpecker
- Greater Racket-tailed Drongo

These birds are associated with young secondary forests, but still require ecological connectivity to other habitats, and also require a sizable forest to survive. These birds may not do well in exotic-dominated secondary forests, or very small forest patches.

## Forest Succession

Adinandra belukar habitats enter a form of "stasis" where they struggle to succeed to the next step, which is the mature lowland forest. Young secondary forests are supposed to, over centuries, transform or succeed into more mature forests of even greater ecological complexity. But the [Tiup-tiup](/entries/plants/coastal/adinandra/dumosa/) and [Resam Fern](/entries/plants/tracheophyta/polypodiopsida/gleicheniales/gleicheniaceae/dicranopteris/linearis/) hinder this succession process.

The [Resam Fern](/entries/plants/tracheophyta/polypodiopsida/gleicheniales/gleicheniaceae/dicranopteris/linearis/) is said to have allelopathy, where it releases toxins through its roots to ensure that the substrate can only be tolerated by its own kind.
