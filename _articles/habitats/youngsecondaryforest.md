There are two kinds of young secondary forest. They can be classified as [exotic-dominated secondary forest](/articles/habitats/exotic-dominated-secondary-forest), or as [native-dominated secondary forest](/articles/habitats/exotic-dominated-secondary-forest).

## Exotic, or Native?

There are two main types of young secondary forest. Exotic-dominated Secondary Forest, or Native-dominated Secondary Forest. Other young secondary forest types like Abandoned Land Forest or Albizia Woodland are more specific classifications of these two main types.

---

## Exotic-dominated Secondary Forest

This young secondary forest type is by far the most common. These forests appear and have an opportunity to grow only when the plot of land was cleared after the 1980s.

### Plants Commonly Found Here

- Falcataria falcata
- Leucaena leucocephala

### Animals Commonly Found Here

- Asian Glossy Starling
- 

### Forest Succession

Exotic-dominated secondary forests come just after the scrubland or grassland stage. This forest type, however, hinders succession by introducing immense amounts of nitrates to ensure only the dominant legumes can survive in the future.

Our lowland forests are nutrient poor, and do not utilise nitrates in the soil. But, the legumes introduce nitrates, a nutrient which they utilise to grow. This allows their children to have a nutrient advantage to outcompete against our native flora.

---

## Native-dominated Secondary Forest

This forest type appears when the land was left untouched since before 1980s, which usually means that the land-use was for fruit orchards, rubber plantations, or abandoned kampungs, which usually results in easy succession due to little perserverance of non-native trees.

### Plants Commonly Found Here

- Smilax setosa
- Fragraea fragrans
- Campnosperma auriculatum
- Macaranga gigantea
- Macaranga heynei

### Animals Commonly Found Here

- Pin-striped Tit Babbler
- Asian Glossy Starling
- Banded Woodpecker
- Greater Racket-tailed Drongo

### Forest Succession

Native-dominated secondary forests can range from the early succession to late-succession range.

## Early Succession Plants

- Macaranga spp.
- Syzygium cerasiforme
- Campnosperma auriculatum

This is the most common type of Native-dominated Secondary Forest. Earliest in succession, the usual age of these forests are 30 years old in Singapore.

## Mid-succession Plants

- Prunus polystachya
- Oncosperma spp.
- Campnosperma squamatum
- Parkia speciosa

Commonly found in areas like the outer edges of the Central Catchment Nature Reserve.

## Late-succession Plants

- Syzygium ridleyi
- Dipterocarpaceae spp.
- Conneraceae spp.

Very hard to come by in Singapore. This is found around the Macritchie Nature Trail, Bukit Timah Nature Reserve, and on Pulau Tekong.
