## Unique Circumstances

Freshwater swamp forests are very unique, where they always have waterlogged soil requiring the flora within them to adapt. Most plants take in oxygen through the roots, and waterlogging hinders this process. Thus, adaptations have been conjured so these plants can respire in other areas through the atmosphere.

Similar to mangroves, the trees within these habitats adapted to have special organs to allow them to respire.

- prop/stilt roots
- lenticels
- pneumatophores

These species are known as freshwater swamp forest **obligates**, which means they live only in the freshwater swamp forest. They would dry up in normal lowland forest areas, and lowland forest species would drown in a freshwater swamp forest.

### Other **associated** Plants

- Syzygium cerasiforme
- Campnosperma auriculatum

These species are known as freshwater swamp forest **associates**, which means they can live in a freshwater swamp forest, but also in other areas. *Sygyzium cerasiforme*, for example, occurs along the Pan-Island Expressway (PIE) as tall hedges, but can also live within freshwater swamp forests without drowning.

## Animals Commonly Found Here

- Birds
    - Chestnut-winged Babbler
    - White-chested Babbler
    - Short-tailed Babbler
- Fishes
    - Whitespot Panchax
    - Dwarf Rasbora
    - Harlequin Rasbora
    - Einthoven's Rasbora
    - Twospot Rasbora
    - Spanner Barb

These birds are always within vicinity of a freshwater swamp forest, but the Chestnut-winged Babbler lives exclusively in the undergrowth of a freshwater swamp forest. They do not explore much into the surrounding lowland forests. Keen to see them? Go explore any freshwater swamp forest habitats around Sime Track in the early morning!

Fishes of the freshwater swamp forest also have to adapt to the absurdly low pH of the water. Due to a high tannin content and low water hardiness (due to lack of exposed rocks), the water pH ranges between 4.6 to 5.5.

## Forest Succession

Freshwater swamp forests are very selective in nature, and only allow freshwater swamp forest obligates or associates to survive. Thus, similar to mangroves, freshwater swamp forests do not have any succession stages.
