## Unique Circumstances

![The canopy of a primary coastal forest in Datai Bay, Langkawi.](https://images.naturehungry.sg/photos/Habitats/CoastalForest/CoastalForest.jpg)

Coastal vegetation is its own kind of forest. Singapore used to have primary coastal forests sprinkled along its coastlines where cliffs would be. However, most of these coastal forests have been destroyed due to human activity.

Coastal forests form when they:
- experience high amounts of constant wind
- are near the coastline

Coastal vegetation have adapted to live in windy conditions, which are very different from our [lowland](/articles/habitats/lowlandforest/) forests, which have adapted to live in very shaded and humid environments. They adapt by either growing very fast to rapidly replace limbs lost to windshear, or by growing to be flexible and tough to resist fracturing.

## Plants Commonly Found Here
- [Jambu Air Laut (*Syzygium grande*)](/entries/plants/coastal/syzygium/grande/)
- [Ketapang (*Terminala catappa*)](/entries/plants/coastal/terminalia/catappa/)
- [Casuarina (*Casuarina equisetifolia*)](/entries/plants/coastal/casuarina/equisetifolia/)

These plants, if covering the land in abundance, would tell you that you are standing in a coastal forest.

### Animals Commonly Found Here

- Plantain Squirrels
- Greater Racket-tailed Drongo
- Banded Woodpecker

Forest-dependent or forest-adapted species found in native-dominated secondary forests would also be found here. These animals are usually travelling from or to other lowland habitats like the mangroves or lowland forests.

### Coastal **Associated** Animals

- Pied Imperial Pigeon
- Pink-necked Green Pigeon
- Asian Red-eyed Bulbul

Coastal forest animals are strong fliers, as flying is the main mode of transport between islands or coastal forests. Examples where coastal island-hopping happens often is in Polynesia, the Philippines, or Indonesia, where small islands support a singular main population.
