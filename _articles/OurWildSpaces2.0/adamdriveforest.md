## Ecological Significance

{% include justifiedgallery.html items=page.galleries.gallery1 %}

The dominant habitat type of Adam Drive Forest is **abandoned land forest**, with large native trees potentially dispersed from the Central Catchment Nature Reserve next to it. Its proximity to the CCNR allows it to serve as a **buffer** zone, and keystone plant species such as the Common Red-stem Fig and Malayan Banyan support a wide variety of fauna when they bear fruit. As such, the site is of high ecological value.

Besides, the Critically Endangered Straw-headed Bulbul, Banded Woodpecker and Greater Racket-tailed Drongo were observed. Frugivorous birds were also present, including the Lineated Barbet. Asian Glossy Starling, and Common Hill Myna, all of which may act as seed dispersers for native tree species and contribute to forest regeneration.

![](https://images.naturehungry.sg/photos/OWS2/Adam-Drive-4.jpg)

In addition, the presence of the Sunda Colugo, whose strongholds are the Central Catchment Nature Reserve and the Bukit Timah Nature Reserve, indicates that the area is important for wildlife connectivity. Multiple streams run across the forest patch that may connect to either Bukit Brown or the CCNR.

## Heritage Significance

{% include justifiedgallery.html items=page.galleries.gallery2 %}

A boundary marker is present in the forested areas of Adam Drive, that holds historical significance and sheds light on the location’s history as part of the British Sime Road Camp, which served as the combined operational headquarters of the Royal Air Force and Army. The camp’s position is adjacent to freshwater supply from Macritchie reservoir, which made it a key battleground in the fall of Singapore. The camp was demolished after the Japanese occupation, where it had served as an internment facility. Today, the only remaining structures from the colonial-era are the black-and-white houses along Adam Drive.

## Why is This Area Threatened?

![](https://images.naturehungry.sg/photos/OWS2/Adam-Drive-7.jpg)

The forested area at Adam Drive is designated for Residential use. With the Singapore Island Country Club’s lease expiring in 2030, This opens up a large swath of land directly adjacent to the Central Catchment Nature Reserve for development.

## Our Hopes for the Site

{% include justifiedgallery.html items=page.galleries.gallery3 %}

Development should be carried out sensitively, such that vegetation could be retained at the fringe of the golf course north of Adam Road Forest. The east of Adam Road Forest should also be retained to preserve connectivity from the Central Catchment southwards and into Bukit Brown, in accordance with NParks' **Ecological Profiling Exercise** (pending findings from any future Environmental Impact Assessments in the area). Finally, the streams in the site could be naturalised, and streams with species of **Conservation Significance** retained.
