## Ecological Significance

![](https://images.naturehungry.sg/photos/OWS2/Toh-Tuck-2.jpg)

Toh Tuck Forest (and Sleepy Valley to its immediate south) sit on an ecological corridor from Bukit Timah to the Southern Ridges through Maju forest, also potentially linking to Jurong Lake Gardens in the west. The habitat comprises **young secondary forest** with **pioneer tree species**, and hills and valleys that become swampy during flooding. Sleepy Valley is considered an **abandoned land forest**, with the presence of oil palms and kampung trees such as durian and longan suggesting cultivation in the past.

While the forest may not be as ecologically diverse or significant as some other areas in this report, the size and proximity to source habitats make Toh Tuck forest and Sleepy Valley key sites for preserving the already fraught connectivity between the CCNR and the southwest of Singapore.

Toh Tuck Forest and Sleepy Valley are both currently marked as Reserve sites on the 2025 URA Master Plan with no immediate plans for development.

## Heritage Significance

Sleepy Valley was the site of a massacre during the Battle of Singapore in World War II. British forces retreating towards Ulu Pandan were caught in the open by Japanese forces that had overtaken them, and only 400 out of 1500 soldiers managed to escape towards Ulu Pandan. There are also remnants of military installations, such as a crumbling pillbox and overgrown trenches.

## Our Hopes for the Site

A concrete canal currently runs through Toh Tuck and Sleepy Valley, which could be naturalised to improve **ecological connectivity** through the area. A 200m-wide connectivity corridor should be retained even if future developments are planned, to retain **ecological connectivity**. **Culverts** linking Toh Tuck to Bukit Batok Nature Park and Sleepy Valley to Maju Forest could also be used to allow movement of fauna like wild boar and pangolin between forest sites.
