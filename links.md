<details>
  <summary><strong>Online versions of printed guidebooks</strong></summary>
  <div class="collapsible-content">
    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://mangrove.nus.edu.sg/pub/seashore/">Seashore Life of Singapore</a><br />
    A full on-line version of the complete BP-Science Centre guide by Leo W H Tan and Peter K L Ng. Includes fact sheets on the flora and fauna of Singapore seashores, an introduction to the shore environment in general, Singapore shores to visit, preparing for a visit, dangerous animals, introduction to special communities on the shore, the strand line, symbiosis Intro and Man and the Sea.</p>

    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://mangrove.nus.edu.sg/guidebooks/index">Mangroves of Singapore</a><br />
    A full on-line version of the complete BP-Science Centre guide by Peter K L Ng and N. Sivasothi (eds.). Includes fact sheets on the flora and fauna of Singapore mangroves, an introduction to mangroves in general and mangroves in Singapore, history and biodiversity, mangroves in Singapore to visit (Pasir Ris and Sungei Buloh), mangrove conservation in Singapore, general introduction to the mangrove ecosystem (abiotic and biotic components), economic value of mangroves.</p>

    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://habitatnews.nus.edu.sg/guidebooks/marinefish/">Marine Fishes of Singapore</a><br />
    A full on-line version of the complete BP-Science Centre guide by Kelvin K P Lim and Jeffrey K Y Low. Includes fact sheets on common marine fishes of Singapore, an introduction to coastal marine habitats, fishy trivia, dangerous fishes, fishes and man.</p>

    
  </div>
</details>


<details>
  <summary><strong>Other Sites</strong></summary>
  <div class="collapsible-content">
    <h3>Seaweeds</h3>
    <p> <a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://www.algaebase.org/">AlgaeBase</a><br />
    detailed and technical factsheets on every algae you can think of. Many with photos.</p>

    <h3>Shore Creatures in General</h3>
    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://www.mesa.edu.au/friends/seashores/page1.html">Life on Australian Seashores</a><br />
    by Keith Davey on the Marine Education Society of Australia website: Fact sheets with photos, diagrams, and even animated gifs with details on various species found in Australia.</p>

    
    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://thescyphozoan.ucmerced.edu/">The Scyphozoan website</a><br />
    by Dr Michael N Dawson: lots of info about jellyfishes in Class Scyphozoa.</p>

    <h3>Molluscs</h3>
    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://manandmollusc.net/">Man and Mollusc/Mollusk</a><br />
    The Resource Site for Students, Educators, and anyone wanting to learn about molluscs. Includes an introduction to the major classes, molluscs as food, medicine, other uses, religious significance, kid's zone, teacher's zone.</p>

    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://seashellsofnsw.org.au">The Seashells of New South Wales</a><br />
    by Des Beechey Research Associate, Australian Museum: family introductions with photos of shells and detailed fact sheets for many species.</p>

    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://www.seaslugforum.net/">The Sea Slug Forum</a><br />
    by Dr Bill Rudman: everything you could possibly want to know about slugs with fabulous photos and close-ups of slug body parts, larvae and more. And lots of links. Go to the General Topics section and browse the many interesting articles.</p>

    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://slugsite.us/">The Slug Site</a><br />
    with a free newsletter and BOW (Branch of the Week), photo and fact sheets of all kinds of opisthobranchs.</p>

    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://www.thecephalopodpage.org/">The Cephalopod Page</a><br />
    by Dr James B Wood on the Dalhousie University and the University of Texas Medical Branch website: everything you could possibly want to know about cephalopods. Lots of info, photos, articles and links to cephalopods. Lots of fabulous photos, a delightful and exhaustive FAQ, and lots of cool articles for the laymen, as well as lesson plans.</p>

    <h3>Echinoderms</h3>
    
    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://echinoblog.blogspot.com/">The Echinoblog</a><br />
    by ChrisM: all about echinoderms with many interesting scientific details and wacky stories, video clips and more.</p>

    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://www.marinespecies.org/Asteroidea/">Wold Asteroidea Database</a><br />
    by Christopher Mah: taxonomic database.</p>

    <h3>Fishes</h3>
    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://www.fishbase.org/">FishBase</a><br />
    technical fact sheets on almost all families and individual species with photos and details on environment, climate, economic importance, resilience, distribution, biology, red list status, whether dangerous.</p>

    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://www.amonline.net.au/fishes/fishfacts/">Australian Museum Fish Site</a> with find-a-fish section which links to factsheets with photos</p>

    <h3>Ascidians</h3>
    <p><a rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow" target="_blank" href="http://www.ascidians.com/">The Dutch Ascidians Homepage</a> by Arjan Gittenberger: general introduction and photos of a wide range of ascidians.</p>
